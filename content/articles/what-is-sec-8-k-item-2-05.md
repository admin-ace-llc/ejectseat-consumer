When a US public company decides to cut jobs, close sites or exit a business, it often has to tell investors within days. The place it does that is **Item 2.05 of Form 8-K**, titled "Costs Associated with Exit or Disposal Activities". It is the closest thing to an official, timestamped layoff announcement that exists.

## What triggers an Item 2.05 filing

The rule applies when the company's board, or an authorized officer, commits to an exit or disposal plan and the company expects to book a material charge under accounting rules for exit costs. The 8-K is due within four business days of that commitment.

The filing is expected to describe the course of action, the expected completion date, and the estimated costs, broken out by type such as severance and other termination benefits. If the company cannot yet estimate the charge, it says so and must file an amendment within four business days of making the estimate.

## How to read one

Most Item 2.05 filings are short. The useful parts are usually in a single paragraph:

- **Share of workforce.** Many filings say "approximately X% of the company's workforce". Others give a headcount instead.
- **Charges.** Look for a range, for example "between $40 million and $50 million", and for the split between cash and non-cash costs.
- **Timing.** Companies state when they expect the plan to be substantially complete, often within a fiscal year.

## What Item 2.05 does not tell you

- **It is not every layoff.** Small reductions that do not produce a material charge may never be filed. Private companies do not file at all.
- **Not every 2.05 is a layoff.** Closing stores, exiting a product line or consolidating facilities also files under this item. EjectSeat labels each filing as a workforce reduction or a restructuring so you can tell them apart.
- **Numbers are the company's own.** Percentages are often approximate, and the final count can differ from the first announcement.
