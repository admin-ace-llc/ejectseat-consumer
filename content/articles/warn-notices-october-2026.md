## What is a WARN notice, and why does it matter?

The federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act requires many larger employers to give advance written notice before a plant closing or mass layoff. The U.S. Department of Labor explains the basics, including which employers are covered, on its [WARN Act page](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/layoffs/warn). Some states add their own rules, so coverage and notice periods vary by state and by employer.

Because notices are public, they are often an early signal of local job losses. They are not the same as an SEC filing. A public company's restructuring may appear first in an 8-K under Item 2.05, while a WARN notice is tied to a specific site and state. For a walkthrough of the fields in a notice, see [how to read a WARN notice](/insights/how-to-read-a-warn-notice), and for the SEC side, see [what SEC 8-K Item 2.05 means](/insights/what-is-sec-8-k-item-2-05).

## What do these notices not tell us?

A WARN listing does not say why an employer is cutting jobs, whether every position will actually be eliminated, or whether some employees will be offered other roles. It also does not describe severance or other benefits. Where a company is not publicly traded, there is usually no SEC filing to compare against.

Notice dates are the dates as listed on the EjectSeat page. Check the original state filing for the official notice date and the date jobs end.

## What should affected workers do?

Workers who receive a notice can check their rights with their state labor department and the Department of Labor page above, since benefits and notice rules vary by state and employer. EjectSeat also has a [layoff readiness check](/check.html) for thinking through your own situation.
