If you were laid off, you file for unemployment insurance (UI) with the state where you worked, and you should do it soon after your last day. According to the U.S. Department of Labor, it generally takes two to three weeks after you file to receive a first benefit check. Rules, amounts and timing differ by state, so treat this guide as a starting point and confirm details with your state agency.

## Who runs unemployment insurance?

UI is a joint state and federal effort. The [U.S. Department of Labor](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/unemployment-insurance) explains that each state administers its own separate program, following federal guidelines. That is why a coworker in another state may get a different answer than you do.

Key points:

- **Where to file:** the state where you worked, not necessarily where you live now.
- **How to file:** in person, by phone or online, depending on the state.
- **Who decides:** your state workforce agency, not your former employer.

## How do I file for unemployment after a layoff?

Start with the DOL page on [how to file for unemployment insurance](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/unemployment-insurance), which points you to your state program. [CareerOneStop](https://www.careeronestop.org/HowTo/AfterALayoff/apply-for-unemployment-benefits.aspx), a Labor Department sponsored site, also has an after-a-layoff walkthrough and a state benefits finder.

If you worked in more than one state, or in a different state from the one where you live, the DOL says your home state's agency can explain how to file in the other state.

## Am I eligible for unemployment benefits?

Eligibility is set by each state, but the DOL describes the common basics:

- You generally must be unemployed through no fault of your own. In most states that means you left your last job because work was no longer available, which is the situation in a layoff.
- You must meet wage or work-time requirements during a "base period." In most states this is roughly the first four of the last five completed calendar quarters before you file.
- You must meet any additional requirements your state sets.

A layoff tied to a company restructuring is usually a separation for lack of work. Your state agency still makes the final call, so answer its questions accurately and keep your separation paperwork.

## How long until the first payment?

The DOL says it generally takes two to three weeks after you file to receive your first benefit check. Filing late does not help, so file as soon as you know your last day, and check whether your state lets you file before that date.

This page does not state how long benefits last or how they are taxed. Both vary, so ask your state agency and check the IRS guidance on unemployment compensation before you plan your budget.

## What to watch

State agencies update their rules, systems and contact options, so use the official links above rather than third-party summaries. If a figure matters to your decision, such as weekly benefit amount or maximum duration, get it from your state agency in writing or from its website.
