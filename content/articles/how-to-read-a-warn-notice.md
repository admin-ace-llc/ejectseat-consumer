The federal **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act** requires many employers to give notice before a mass layoff or plant closing. States publish the notices they receive, which makes them one of the few public sources that includes **private** companies.

## Who has to file

Broadly, the federal law covers employers with 100 or more employees. It requires 60 days' notice for a plant closing or a mass layoff, which generally means job losses at a single site that reach set thresholds, such as 50 or more employees making up at least a third of that site's workforce, or 500 or more employees. Some states have their own, stricter laws with lower thresholds and longer notice periods.

## What a notice usually contains

- The employer's name and the site address
- The number of affected employees
- The planned date of the layoffs or closure
- Whether the action is a layoff or a closure

## What to keep in mind

- **A notice is a site, not a company.** Ten notices from ten sites can add up to one company-wide cut.
- **The date is the effective date.** The notice usually appears roughly 60 days before it.
- **Remote workers may be missing.** Employees not tied to a worksite can fall outside what the notice counts.
- **Coverage varies by state.** Not every state publishes in a format that is easy to read.

## Using WARN with SEC filings

Public companies can show up in both places. An SEC [Item 2.05 filing](/insights/what-is-sec-8-k-item-2-05) gives the company-wide plan and charge; WARN notices show where the cuts land.
